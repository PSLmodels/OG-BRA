# Taxes in OG-BRA

The government is not an optimizing agent in `OG-BRA`. The government levies taxes on household income, corporate income, and value added. With these resources, the government provides transfers to households, spends resources on public goods, and makes rule-based adjustments to stabilize the economy in the long-run. The government can run budget deficits or surpluses in a given year and must, therefore, be able to accumulate debt or savings.  The spending and debt parameters are discussed in Chapter {ref}`Chap_MacroCalib`.  Taxes are discussed in this chapter.


## Personal income taxes
The government sector influences households through two terms in the household budget constraint {eq}`EqHHBC`---government transfers $TR_{t}$ and through the total tax liability function $T_{s,t}$, which can be decomposed into the effective tax rate times total income {eq}`EqTaxCalcLiabETR2`. In this chapter, we detail the household tax component of government activity $T_{s,t}$ in `OG-BRA`.

```{math}
:label: EqHHBC
  c_{j,s,t} + b_{j,s+1,t+1} &= (1 + r_{hh,t})b_{j,s,t} + w_t e_{j,s} n_{j,s,t} + \\
  &\quad\quad\zeta_{j,s}\frac{BQ_t}{\lambda_j\omega_{s,t}} + \eta_{j,s,t}\frac{TR_{t}}{\lambda_j\omega_{s,t}} + ubi_{j,s,t} - T_{s,t}  \\
  &\quad\forall j,t\quad\text{and}\quad s\geq E+1 \quad\text{where}\quad b_{j,E+1,t}=0\quad\forall j,t
```

The total tax function, $T_{s,t}$, is a function of personal income taxes, taxes on bequests, and wealth taxes.  In the default calibration, wealth taxes are set to zero in `OG-BRA`, and bequests are taxed at the rate `tau_bq = 0.04` (see the bequest tax section below). Personal income taxes are modeled as linear taxes set to average effective and marginal tax rates.  The [OG-Core documentation](https://pslmodels.github.io/OG-Core/content/theory/government.html#taxes) details more detailed ways to match the progressivity of the tax system; given the available Brazilian data we use linear rates, calibrated from the Receita Federal's DIRPF tables as described below.

Brazil's individual income tax (IRPF) has five statutory brackets ranging from 0% to 27.5%. We calibrate the three linear rates from the Receita Federal's [Grandes Números DIRPF](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/dados-abertos/grandes-numeros-do-irpf) tables for calendar year 2024 (41.7 million declarations, aggregated over the five tax-base brackets):

- **`etr_params` = 0.07.** Tax due at adjustment was R\$349bn on R\$3,086bn of taxable income (an 11.3% effective rate), but filers also declared R\$633bn taxed exclusively at source (roughly 17.5% withholding on top of the R\$349bn) and R\$2,370bn of exempt income — mostly dividends, which were untaxed in 2024. Total income tax over total declared income is about 7%, and the model's effective rate applies to all household income, so we use 0.07. (Anchoring instead to aggregate revenue over GDP would give about 0.05, because informal income never enters the declared base; anchoring to taxable income only would give 0.11. We take the middle concept: the average burden on a formal household's full income.)
- **`mtrx_params` = 0.22**, the taxable-income-weighted average of the statutory bracket rates (64% of taxable income is declared in the top 27.5% bracket).
- **`mtry_params` = 0.12**, the composition-weighted marginal rate on declared capital income: dividends (R\$813bn, taxed at 10% from 2026 under Law No. 15,270/2025), exclusive-taxation fixed income (R\$466bn at ~17.5%), rentals (R\$47bn at the 27.5% margin), and exempt savings accounts (R\$120bn). Under pre-2026 law (dividends exempt) the same calculation gives 0.065.

Two of the choices above can be checked against independent evidence. First, the 17.5% withholding imputation on exclusive-taxation income: the Receita Federal's collection reports show total IRRF on capital income of R\$152.8bn in 2024 ([Análise da Arrecadação](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/relatorios/arrecadacao-federal)); our imputed household withholding (0.175 × R\$466bn ≈ R\$82bn) is 53% of that total, a plausible household share given that the remainder falls on corporate holders and investment funds. The statutory schedule runs from 22.5% (holdings under six months) to 15% (over two years, and the 2023 fund-taxation law's rate), so household portfolios tilted to longer horizons put the effective rate between 15% and 17.5%. Second, the choice of the formal-filer income concept: IPEA's progressivity diagnostics ([Carta de Conjuntura, October 2024](https://www.ipea.gov.br/cartadeconjuntura/index.php/2024/10/progressividade-tributaria-diagnostico-para-uma-proposta-de-reforma/)) report effective IRPF rates peaking around 12% at monthly incomes near R\$30,000 and falling at the top from the dividend exemption — a profile whose income-weighted average is consistent with our 7% on declared income. Computed economy-wide instead (household-attributable income tax of roughly R\$404bn over total household income of roughly 80% of GDP), the effective rate is about 4.5%, the informality wedge: filers hold about 65% of household income and pay essentially all of the income tax. We use the formal-filer concept because the rest of the calibration (notably the statutory `tau_payroll` = 0.28) also describes the formal economy; an explicit informality treatment would need to adjust both together.

Law No. 15,270/2025 also raises the full exemption threshold to R\$5,000 per month from January 2026 and introduces a minimum tax for annual incomes above R\$600,000; the effective rates above are measured on 2024 declarations and should be re-computed when calendar-2026 data is published. Source: [Grandes Números IRPF 2025, ano-calendário 2024](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/estudos/imposto-de-renda/estudos-por-ano/grandes-numeros-do-IRPF-2008-a-2025); [Trench Rossi Watanabe — Law 15,270/2025](https://www.trenchrossi.com/en/legal-alerts/brazil-enacts-law-15270-2025-which-taxes-dividends-and-amend-personal-income-tax-rules/).

## Bequest taxes

Inheritances and gifts in Brazil are taxed by the states through the ITCMD (Imposto sobre Transmissão Causa Mortis e Doação). The Federal Senate caps the rate at 8% nationally, and state rates range from 2% to 8%. Constitutional Amendment 132/2023 and Complementary Law 227 (sanctioned in late 2025) require all states to adopt progressive schedules from 2026; São Paulo, for example, moved from a flat 4% rate to a progressive 2–8% schedule. `OG-BRA` models the bequest tax as a flat rate and sets `tau_bq = 0.04` — the midpoint of state schedules and São Paulo's long-standing flat rate — as a stylized economy-wide effective rate, noting that exemption thresholds keep the average effective rate well below the 8% ceiling. Source: [NR Advocacia — Alíquota ITCMD por estado (2026)](https://nradvocacia.com.br/aliquota-itcmd-por-estado/); [Colégio Notarial do Brasil — Novas regras para ITCMD e ITBI](https://www.notariado.org.br/novas-regras-para-itcmd-e-itbi-entram-em-vigor-e-podem-deixar-herancas-doacoes-e-compras-de-imoveis-mais-caras/).

## Payroll taxes

Brazil's payroll tax system is administered through the National Social Security Institute (INSS) and the Government Severance Fund (FGTS). Employers are required to contribute 20% of gross wages to INSS and an additional 8% to FGTS, for a combined employer payroll tax burden of **28%** (`tau_payroll = 0.28`). Employees also pay progressive INSS contributions ranging from 7.5% to 14% of their wages, capped at approximately R\$952 per month in 2025. Source: [Vialto Partners — Brazil Social Security 2025](https://vialtopartners.com/regional-alerts/brazil-social-security-updated-social-security-tax-table-for-2025); [Boundless — Payroll Taxes in Brazil](https://boundlesshq.com/guides/brazil/taxes/).

### Split of income and payroll tax revenue

For revenue reporting, OG-Core splits the combined household income and payroll tax take with the parameter `frac_tax_payroll`, the fraction attributable to payroll taxes. We set `frac_tax_payroll = 0.57`, computed from Receita Federal collection data ([arrecadação por divisão econômica da CNAE](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/dados-abertos/receitadata/arrecadacao/arrecadacao-por-divisao-economica-da-cnae)) as social contributions (Contribuição Previdenciária plus CPSSS) over the sum of those contributions and personal income taxes (IRPF plus IRRF), averaged over 2022–2025 (the ratio is stable at 0.56–0.58). Two offsetting approximations: IRRF includes some withholding on capital income (which overstates the income-tax denominator), and FGTS contributions are not administered by the Receita Federal and so are absent from the payroll numerator.

## Corporate income taxes

`OG-BRA` uses a combined statutory corporate income tax rate of **34%** (`cit_rate = 0.34`), comprising the Corporate Income Tax (IRPJ) at 25% (a 15% base rate plus a 10% surtax on annual taxable profits exceeding R\$240,000) and the Social Contribution on Net Profit (CSLL) at 9%. This combined rate has been stable and applies to most non-financial businesses. Financial institutions face higher CSLL rates (15–20%), yielding combined rates of 40–45%. Source: [PwC Brazil Corporate Tax Summary](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/brazil/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income).

## Value-added taxes

Brazil does not currently have a unified VAT. Its indirect tax system is composed of multiple overlapping federal, state, and municipal levies. The two main federal consumption taxes are PIS (Programa de Integração Social, 1.65%) and COFINS (Contribuição para o Financiamento da Seguridade Social, 7.6%), which together total approximately 9.25% on revenues. State ICMS rates range from 17–20%, and municipal ISS applies at 2–5% on services.

`OG-BRA` sets `tau_c = 0.13`, the effective indirect tax rate on household consumption computed from Brazil's national accounts. Using the annual input-output tables for Brazil estimated by Alves-Passoni and Freitas (2023) from the IBGE Supply and Use Tables, we compute the ratio of taxes on products (net of subsidies) embedded in household final consumption to household consumption valued net of those taxes. This effective rate is stable across 2015–2021, ranging from 0.124 to 0.133 (0.127 in 2018 and 0.133 in 2021). Because it is measured from taxes actually collected, this rate captures the combined incidence of all indirect taxes (ICMS, PIS, COFINS, IPI, ISS) net of exemptions and informality, which a statutory-rate approach would overstate — while using only the federal PIS + COFINS rate (approximately 9%) would omit the state and municipal taxes that make up the majority of Brazil's indirect tax burden. Source: Alves-Passoni, P. and F. Freitas (2023), "Estimação de Matrizes Insumo-Produto anuais para o Brasil no Sistema de Contas Nacionais: Referência 2010", *Pesquisa e Planejamento Econômico* 53(1), [electronic appendix](https://repositorio.ipea.gov.br/handle/11058/13345).

Brazil is currently undertaking a major indirect tax reform (Constitutional Amendment 132/2023) that will replace PIS, COFINS, ICMS, IPI, and ISS with a dual VAT system consisting of a federal CBS (Contribuição sobre Bens e Serviços, targeting ~8.8%) and a state/municipal IBS (Imposto sobre Bens e Serviços, targeting ~17.7%) for a combined rate of approximately 26.5%. The transition runs from 2026 through 2033, with CBS becoming fully operational in 2027. The `tau_c` parameter should be revisited as the reform proceeds. Source: [PwC Brazil Corporate Other Taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/brazil/corporate/other-taxes); [TaxUp — Brazil Tax Reform Guide 2026–2033](https://taxup.com.br/en/solutions/tax-reform/).
